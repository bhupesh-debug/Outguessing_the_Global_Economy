Outguessing the Global Economy

Where global money is moving, why it is moving, where it is likely to move next — and which asset has the highest probability of outperforming. An interactive companion to the Part 18 research article. Data as of November 7, 2026; market scores dated November 6, 2026.

⚠ The backtest failed — read this before using anything on this page. The 10-year walk-forward backtest of the scoring method (Appendix A of the paper) does not validate it: the top-ranked asset beat the peer median in only 17.4% of 12-month windows (n=109) vs ~50% for a random pick, and its top-1 portfolio compounded at −1.71% a year vs +8.07% for equal-weight. The November 6 scores below are judgements with a warning attached, not validated signals. Every number on this page is a model estimate. This is research and education, not investment advice.

4 · Monte Carlo simulator — run it yourself

10,000 draws, fixed seed 20261107 (same result every run). Returns are drawn from a Normal distribution with each asset's observed 12-month mean/SD from Table 7, shifted by the selected scenario (±0.5 SD; Blended mixes the three by their probabilities). P(outperform) = share of draws beating the cross-sectional median of the seven assets — the same definition Table 8 uses. Draws are independent across assets: a simplification, stated openly — see the comparison with the paper's empirical frequencies below the results.

What would prove this view wrong? All three together, by end-November 2026: October US CPI hot enough on November 10 to lock in a December hike; Brent crude above $110 a barrel; and FPI flows into Indian equities negative again in November. Any one weakens the thesis; all three invalidate it. Staleness: October US CPI prints November 10 and the Fed meets December 8–9, 2026 — either can change the regime, and these scores have not been re-run on fresh data.
