# Recommendation — What To Do, What To Avoid, Conclusion

Source: Part 18 v2 (Sections 9–11, Part 19) and the condensed edition.
Data as of November 7, 2026. This is research and education, not
investment advice. No reader should act on it without independent
verification.

## The recommendation — framed as a judgement with a warning

**Use this framework to read the market. Do not trade on its ranking.**

That is the honest recommendation, because the two halves of the paper
point in opposite directions and both must be carried:

1. **The judgemental ranking** says Indian equities lead (59/100), the
   dollar is second (58), gold third (57), and the rotation — out of
   crowded US growth, into dollars and short-duration safety, selectively
   into India — has begun. The direction of the rotation is the thing the
   model is most confident about, and the early-rotation signal (FPI
   buying ~$1.2bn in late October before the Nifty's +2.01% surge) is the
   strongest single signal in the article.
2. **The backtest** says the mechanical version of the method does not
   predict: top-1 hit rate 17.4% at 12 months, top-1 portfolio −1.71% a
   year vs +8.07% equal-weight, rank IC −0.202. The calibrated
   frequencies reverse the original probabilities: US equities 77% and
   gold 69% lead at 12 months; India equities sit at 44%.

A recommendation that reported only (1) would be marketing. One that
reported only (2) would discard a framework whose analogue component
(+0.298) and flow discipline have real content. The recommendation is
therefore conditional and dated:

- **If you are a researcher or student of markets:** the framework is
  worth using as a reading tool — scores, flow labels, surprise engine,
  expectation gaps, Bayesian updates — and the re-specification (shrink
  Macro and Relative yield, promote the analogue, real Flow series,
  re-run the backtest) is the next piece of work. That is the named next
  step in the papers, and it is open, not done.
- **If you are allocating capital:** the November 6 scores are judgements
  with a warning attached, not validated signals. The paper's own
  conclusion is that the next step is a re-specification and a fresh
  backtest, "not a trade on the current ranking". Nothing in this project
  changes that.
- **Watch the three dated checkpoints:** October US CPI on November 10,
  November FPI flows (NSDL/CDSL), and the December 8–9 FOMC. They will
  score the thesis within a month — that is the point of stating the
  invalidation signal in advance.

## What to avoid

1. **Do not treat the 59 as a signal.** The Final score is a judgemental
   assessment. Its mechanical proxy failed its first test, and the
   mechanical cross-check on November 6 nearly reverses the ranking
   (dollar 55.2 first, gold 23.1 last).
2. **Do not confuse price with flow.** A rising Nifty is not proof
   capital arrived; the paper's discipline (ACTUAL / PROXY /
   MARKET-INFERRED labels) exists because that confusion is the most
   common error in this field. The India case rests on the domestic bid
   ($75bn), not on the price.
3. **Do not extrapolate the two-week FPI turn.** $1.2bn of buying against
   $31bn of selling is the most fragile assumption in the thesis. A
   two-week turn is not a regime change until November's flows confirm it.
4. **Do not ignore the dollar.** The strongest contrarian thesis is that
   the India thesis is a dollar thesis in disguise: Fed hike + Brent
   above $110 → dollar 105, rupee 97, RBI to 6.00%, FPI reversal. The
   domestic bid has never been tested in a genuine stress episode.
5. **Do not use the v1 probabilities.** The first version's model
   estimates (India equities 62%, Gold 58%, USD 55% at 12 months) were
   systematically inverted. The calibrated frequencies (Table 8) replace
   them.
6. **Do not mistake the Monte Carlo for a forecast.** The simulation in
   this project describes the backtest window's observed return
   distributions under the paper's scenarios. Its regime shifts (±0.5 SD)
   are our assumption, its draws are independent across assets, and its
   inputs are backward-looking.
7. **Do not act on stale data.** The scores are dated November 6–7, 2026.
   A hot CPI print or a December hike changes the regime — re-running the
   scores on fresh data is the only cure, and it has not happened yet.

## Conclusion

Where is global money moving? Out of crowded US growth equities and
precious metals on the day, into the US dollar and short-duration safety,
and selectively — early, tentatively, but demonstrably — into Indian
equities and bonds, carried more by domestic Indian capital than by
foreign capital. Where is it leaving? The 2026 foreign bid for India as
a whole, the gold trade that peaked in January, and the assumption that
the Fed would cut. Why? Because a hot payrolls report repriced the Fed
toward a hike, lifting yields and the dollar, while the same shock made
beaten-down Indian equities the anti-AI trade and the domestic bid held
the market up while foreigners sold.

Which asset has the highest probability of outperforming? Indian equities,
at a final score of 59/100 — the judgemental ranking — with the US dollar
second at 58 and gold third at 57 — and with the honest caveat that the
foreign-flow leg of the India case is the weakest part of it, that the
calibrated frequencies reverse the ranking (US equities 77%, gold 69%,
India equities 44% at 12 months), and that a hot October CPI on November
10 would change the answer.

What data would prove this view wrong? A December hike combined with
Brent above $110 and a fresh FPI selling streak in November — the
invalidation signal, stated in advance, so that the analysis can be
scored rather than defended. The ultimate principle, as the program
states it, is not to predict what the economy will do, but to anticipate
what the participants in the economy will do because of what they expect
the economy to do. On November 6, 2026, the participants are telling us,
in their flows if not yet in their words, that the rotation has begun.
The next two weeks will say whether they meant it.
